I am a Ph.D. student (direct track) in Electrical Engineering at Tel Aviv University, advised by Dan Raviv in the Geometric Deep Learning Lab. My research focuses on the foundations of deep learning: spectral bias, optimization dynamics, and generalization. Treating learned representations as signals, I aim to understand how neural networks learn at different frequencies, and to turn that understanding into principled methods for real-world problems.

Alongside my Ph.D., I am an AI Research Scientist Intern in the Multimodal AI group at IBM Research, led by Eli Schwartz, and I lead the deep learning group at Dtect Vision. Previously, I received a B.Sc. in Applied Mathematics and completed my M.Sc. thesis work in Electrical Engineering (signal processing track), both at Tel Aviv University.

I am always happy to discuss research on frequency-domain analysis of neural networks or detection problems grounded in signal processing. Feel free to reach out via email.

SONAR: Spectral-Contrastive Audio Residuals for Generalizable Deepfake Detection

Ido Nitzan Hidekel, Gal Lifshitz, Khen Cohen, Dan Raviv

International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026

PDF Code Project Page

Neural networks preferentially learn low frequencies, which both leaves high-frequency artifacts in generated audio and leaves those artifacts under-exploited by common detectors. SONAR is a dual-path framework that learns data-driven SRM-style filters to isolate high-frequency residuals and uses a Jensen–Shannon contrastive objective to pull real content/noise pairs together and push fake pairs apart in latent space, yielding strong generalization to unseen generators.

Catastrophic Forgetting is Low-Rank: A Function-Space Theory for Continual Adaptation

Ido Nitzan Hidekel, Dan Raviv

ICML Workshop on Continual Adaptation at Scale: Towards Sustainable AI (CATS), 2026

Paper coming soon

A decade of mitigation strategies later, no theory explains why catastrophic forgetting happens. We give a function-space account in the Neural Tangent Kernel regime: new-task training induces interference along a structured, low-rank subspace of output space defined by the cross-task NTK. A closed-form predictor identifies the forgetting vector (direction and magnitude jointly) before new-task training begins, and the theory shows forgetting concentrates in just 1–6 NTK eigenmodes, explaining why parameter-space methods fail on shared-head benchmarks and motivating a targeted spectral regularizer.
